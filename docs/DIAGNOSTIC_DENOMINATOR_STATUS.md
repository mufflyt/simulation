# Diagnostic denominator status — what is known, what is missing, and why

**Diagnostic only.** Nothing here feeds the production pipeline, alters
`per_entering`, or changes `scientific-readiness`, which remains **BLOCKED**.

Generated by `build_diagnostic_denominator_table(2023L)`. 22 rows.

---

## The question this table exists to keep open

Medicare Part B PUF yields **79,787** practice-new FPMRS office consultations in
2023. The tempting next step is:

```r
per_entering <- 79787 / entering        # DO NOT
```

`pathway_stage_entrants()` sets the conservative stage's `entering` to
`treated[[cond]]` **unchanged**, and `.lifecourse_treated()` builds `treated` as

```
prevalence × recognition × p_seek × p_referral × p_eligible × p_treated
```

which is **2.8–10.7% of prevalence** and already carries the multipliers the
canonical estimand *absorbs*. Dividing an observed consultation count by it
counts recognition, seeking and referral twice.

**The defect is not circularity.** `per_entering` is applied after `entering`
and never feeds back into `treated`; there is no loop. The denominator is simply
a different quantity from the one the estimand names. Calling it circular sends
a reader looking for feedback that does not exist — and finding none, they may
conclude the objection was overblown.

The canonical denominator is the **upstream eligible prevalent stock**:
`prevalence × p_eligible`, and nothing else.

## What is missing, and exactly what would populate it

### 1. Medicare FFS enrolment denominator — 7 rows `BLOCKED`

`missing_reason = missing_cms_ffs_enrollment_denominator`

Required: **women aged 65+ enrolled in Medicare fee-for-service, by age band,
2023**, to pair with the 79,787 numerator.

Nothing on hand supplies it:

| candidate | why it fails |
|---|---|
| MCBS 2022 (`cspuf2022`) | weighted *survey* PUF, wrong year, needs its own transport argument |
| Part B PUF beneficiary counts | these are **users**, not enrollees — using them makes the denominator a function of the numerator, the one genuine circularity available here |
| all-payer national 65+ | answers a different question while looking like an answer to this one |

**Would populate it:** a CMS Medicare enrolment file — the Medicare Enrollment
Dashboard / Beneficiary Enrollment counts by state, age and FFS-vs-MA, or the
MBSF under a CMS DUA.

Until then the cell stays `NA`. That is deliberate.

### 2. Condition split of the 79,787 — structurally impossible from this source

`condition_split_blocked_by = part_b_puf_carries_no_diagnosis_field`

The Part B PUF `by_service` table has **no diagnosis field of any kind** —
columns are NPI, provider attributes, `HCPCS_Cd`, `HCPCS_Desc`,
`Place_Of_Srvc`, and counts/payments. No ICD anywhere.

So the count cannot be split into UI, POP and AI. It is held as a single
aggregate row with `condition = NA`, and the table's tests assert that **exactly
one row** carries it. Allocating it by prevalence share would manufacture three
condition-specific numbers from one, and the canonical blocker needs three
*independently estimated* rates.

**Would populate it:** a claims source carrying diagnosis alongside the
encounter — i.e. the APCD extract itself.

### 3. Condition-specific prevalence below 65 — 9 rows `PARTIAL`

`missing_reason = prevalence_not_condition_specific_below_65`

`pfd_prevalence_by_band()` returns the same values (0.098 / 0.269 / 0.330) for
UI, POP and AI at 20–39, 40–59 and 60–64 — the any-PFD figure. Condition-specific
values exist only at 65–79 and 80+. The rows are emitted rather than dropped so
the limitation is visible.

**Would populate it:** condition-specific prevalence estimates for women under
65 — NHANES for UI, and a POP/AI source that does not currently exist in the
tree.

## What the 79,787 is not

Four constraints belong on the label, and the first two point in opposite
directions:

- **Practice-new, not care-new.** New-patient CPT identifies patients new to a
  *practice*. Someone switching FPMRS practices bills as new — **overcounts**
  first entry.
- **PUF suppression** drops cells below 11 beneficiaries entirely — **undercounts**.
- **Roster FPMRS only** — pelvic-floor care by generalist ObGyn or urology is
  invisible — **undercounts**.
- **Medicare FFS, overwhelmingly 65+** — nothing transports to under-65 without
  a stated assumption.

Because the biases run in both directions, 79,787 is **neither a defensible
lower bound nor an upper bound**. It is an aggregate external plausibility
statistic.

## Naming

Where the aggregate ratio eventually becomes computable it is called

```
medicare_ffs_practice_new_fpmrs_ratio_65plus_2023
```

and never `per_entering` or `annual_first_urps_entry_rate`. Those are canonical
parameters with settled definitions; this is a practice-new consultation ratio in
one payer stratum. A shared name is how a diagnostic gets adopted as an estimate
by inheritance, so a test asserts the name does not collide.

`medicare_ffs_practice_new_fpmrs_ratio_65plus_2023()` returns `ratio = NA_real_`
today, with `status = "BLOCKED"` and the machine-readable reason above.

## Guard

`assert_upstream_denominator()` admits a denominator only when it is explicitly
tagged `upstream_eligible_prevalent_stock` **and** its source names nothing
downstream of the entry process. Declaration is required rather than inferred —
a numeric vector carries no evidence of how it was built, and the whole failure
mode is a downstream quantity that looks numerically fine.

Rejected sources include `treated`, `treated_national`, `.lifecourse_treated`,
`entering`, `pathway_stage_entrants`, `pathway_service_volumes`, stage volumes,
`care_seeking`, `p_referral` and `recognition`. Eleven such strings are asserted
to fail in `test-diagnostic-denominator-table.R`.
