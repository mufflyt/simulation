## The question this table exists to keep open

Medicare Part B PUF yields **79,787** practice-new FPMRS office consultations in
2023. The tempting next step is:

```r
per_entering <- 79787 / entering        # DO NOT
```

`pathway_stage_entrants()` sets the conservative stage's `entering` to
`treated[[cond]]` **unchanged**, and `.lifecourse_treated()` builds `treated` as

```
prevalence × recognition × p_seek × p_referral × p_eligible × p_treated
```

which is **2.8–10.7% of prevalence** and already carries the multipliers the
canonical estimand *absorbs*. Dividing an observed consultation count by it
counts recognition, seeking and referral twice.

**The defect is not circularity.** `per_entering` is applied after `entering`
and never feeds back into `treated`; there is no loop. The denominator is simply
a different quantity from the one the estimand names. Calling it circular sends
a reader looking for feedback that does not exist — and finding none, they may
conclude the objection was overblown.

The canonical denominator is the **upstream eligible prevalent stock**:
`prevalence × p_eligible`, and nothing else.

## What is missing, and exactly what would populate it

### 1. Medicare FFS enrolment denominator — **RESOLVED 2026-08-29**

Was: `missing_reason = missing_cms_ffs_enrollment_denominator`, 7 rows BLOCKED.

**Women aged 65+ in Original Medicare (fee-for-service), 2023 = 16,542,982.**

Source: CMS Program Statistics, Original Medicare Enrollment, Table 12
(`MDCR ENROLL AB 12_CPS_02ENR`). **Public, free, no DUA** — the file was a
50 KB download from data.cms.gov. It did not need the APCD request, and it did
not need anyone's permission.

**It is a published cell, not a marginal times a share.** Table 11 gives age and
sex only as marginals and cannot supply the cross. Table 12 crosses sex with
entitlement type, and "Total Aged" *is* the 65+ category — Table 12's own
"65 Years and Over" row places its entire count in Total Aged and zero in Total
Disabled. Reconciliation, asserted in code rather than eyeballed:

```
  Male   × Aged    14,290,951
+ Female × Aged    16,542,982
= 30,833,933   vs published 65+ total 30,833,932   (differs by 1, rounding)
```

`cms_original_medicare_enrollment_2023()` re-checks that reconciliation on every
call and errors if a vendored cell is ever edited out of agreement.

**One caveat that changes the number.** 16,542,982 counts Part A *and/or* Part B.
The numerator requires Part B. Among 65+, Part B coverage is
25,732,430 / 30,833,932 = **0.83455** — but CMS does not publish that share by
sex, so applying it to the female count is an assumption. Both denominators are
therefore carried, and the ratio is reported three ways rather than one being
chosen silently.

### 2. Condition split of the 79,787 — structurally impossible from this source

`condition_split_blocked_by = part_b_puf_carries_no_diagnosis_field`

The Part B PUF `by_service` table has **no diagnosis field of any kind** —
columns are NPI, provider attributes, `HCPCS_Cd`, `HCPCS_Desc`,
`Place_Of_Srvc`, and counts/payments. No ICD anywhere.

So the count cannot be split into UI, POP and AI. It is held as a single
aggregate row with `condition = NA`, and the table's tests assert that **exactly
one row** carries it. Allocating it by prevalence share would manufacture three
condition-specific numbers from one, and the canonical blocker needs three
*independently estimated* rates.

**Would populate it:** a claims source carrying diagnosis alongside the
encounter — i.e. the APCD extract itself.

### 3. Condition-specific prevalence below 65 — 9 rows `PARTIAL`

`missing_reason = prevalence_not_condition_specific_below_65`

`pfd_prevalence_by_band()` returns the same values (0.098 / 0.269 / 0.330) for
UI, POP and AI at 20–39, 40–59 and 60–64 — the any-PFD figure. Condition-specific
values exist only at 65–79 and 80+. The rows are emitted rather than dropped so
the limitation is visible.

**Would populate it:** condition-specific prevalence estimates for women under
65 — NHANES for UI, and a POP/AI source that does not currently exist in the

## The computed service rate

**79,787 is a count of SERVICES, not of women.** An earlier version of this
document inferred that because `Tot_Srvcs` (79,787) and summed `Tot_Benes`
(79,785) are nearly equal, the total was "effectively one per person". That
inference is wrong. PUF beneficiary counts are computed **within** each
provider/service cell, so their near-equality shows only that a woman rarely
receives the same new-patient code twice from the same provider — which the
billing rules already require. It says nothing about the same woman appearing in
a *different* cell: another NPI, another group, another new-patient code. There
are **1,322 cells across 794 NPIs**, and the PUF supplies no key with which to
detect the overlap.

So every figure below is **services per 1,000 population**, never a per-woman
probability.

| denominator | n | services/1,000 | reading |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| all FFS women 65+ | 16,542,982 | 4.82 | crude — denominator includes women without Part B |
| **Part B female 65+** | **13,805,931** | **5.78** | **primary** |
| any-PFD stock, coverage-unrestricted | 6,622,155 | 12.05 | exploratory |
| Part B ∩ any-PFD stock | 5,526,514 | 14.44 | exploratory |

**The primary rate is 5.78 practice-new FPMRS E/M services per 1,000 Part B
women 65+ per year.** It is primary because numerator and denominator sit on the
same coverage footing and no disease definition is imposed — which matters
precisely because the numerator carries no diagnosis. Its one assumption is that
the published 65+ Part B share (0.83455) applies to women.

The two disease-conditioned rows are **exploratory, not estimand-aligned**. A
previous revision called 12.05 estimand-aligned; that was wrong twice over —
its denominator includes women without Part B, a coverage universe the numerator
cannot arise from, and conditioning a diagnosis-free numerator on disease
prevalence assumes every practice-new FPMRS visit arises from a prevalent PFD
case, which is neither established nor testable here.

### On the any-PFD universe

Checked rather than assumed. At 65+ the value comes from
`mufflyaccess::pfd_prevalence()` and **is a genuine union, not a sum**: 0.368 at
65–79 against UI + POP + FI = 0.473. That is consistent with Nygaard's "at least
one of UI, FI or POP", so FI/AI is included and overlap is handled.

It is **not** the `.PFD_PREVALENCE_BY_BAND` constant in
`R/data-urps_population.R`, whose "UI + POP combined" comment describes a
different local fallback on a different band scheme (18-34/35-44/45-64/65-74/75+)
that is not used at 65+.

**Is 5.78/1,000 a believable universe?** It is the right order of magnitude for
a chronic, under-treated condition where most prevalent disease never reaches a
subspecialist. It is not evidence the model's 65+ arm is right, and not a target
to fit to.

## What the 79,787 is not

Four constraints belong on the label, and the first two point in opposite
directions — which is why the number cannot be rescued as a bound:

- **Practice-new, not care-new.** New-patient CPT identifies patients new to a
  *practice*. Someone switching FPMRS practices bills as new — **overcounts**
  first entry.
- **PUF suppression** drops cells below 11 beneficiaries entirely — **undercounts**.
- **Roster FPMRS only** — pelvic-floor care by generalist ObGyn or urology is
  invisible — **undercounts**.
- **Medicare FFS, 65+** — nothing transports to under-65 without a stated
  assumption, and the under-65 FFS population is the disability cohort, not a
  younger version of this one.

Because the biases run in both directions — and because the numerator cannot
be deduplicated to people at all — these rates are **neither a defensible lower
bound nor an upper bound** on the true entry rate. It is an
aggregate plausibility statistic, and the fact that it is now a real number
rather than an `NA` does not upgrade it.

## Naming

Where the aggregate ratio eventually becomes computable it is called

```
medicare_ffs_practice_new_fpmrs_ratio_65plus_2023
```

and never `per_entering` or `annual_first_urps_entry_rate`. Those are canonical
parameters with settled definitions; this is a practice-new consultation ratio in
one payer stratum. A shared name is how a diagnostic gets adopted as an estimate
by inheritance, so a test asserts the name does not collide.

`medicare_ffs_practice_new_fpmrs_ratio_65plus_2023()` now returns three rows,
one per denominator definition, each carrying its own `status` and a declared
`assumption`. Only the crude denominator is assumption-free.

## Guard

`assert_upstream_denominator()` admits a denominator only when it is explicitly
tagged `upstream_eligible_prevalent_stock` **and** its source names nothing
downstream of the entry process. Declaration is required rather than inferred —
a numeric vector carries no evidence of how it was built, and the whole failure
mode is a downstream quantity that looks numerically fine.

Rejected sources include `treated`, `treated_national`, `.lifecourse_treated`,
`entering`, `pathway_stage_entrants`, `pathway_service_volumes`, stage volumes,
`care_seeking`, `p_referral` and `recognition`. Eleven such strings are asserted
to fail in `test-diagnostic-denominator-table.R`.
